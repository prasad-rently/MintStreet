Bond vs. Fixed Deposit

▶ interactive

Both are ways to lend money instead of owning it — but they pay you back differently. See the same principal, the same rate, two different shapes of growth.

Fixed Deposit — maturity value—
Bond — value if coupons not reinvested—
Difference from compounding—

An FD automatically reinvests its interest, so it compounds. A bond pays its coupon to you in cash each year — what happens next is up to you. Manually reinvest every coupon at the same rate and the two converge; spend the coupons instead, and the FD pulls ahead purely because of what compounding does in the background.

Illustrative only, at a single flat rate for both instruments for comparison. Real FD and bond rates differ by issuer and tenure, and bonds carry credit and interest-rate risk an FD (up to the insured limit) does not. Not investment advice.
